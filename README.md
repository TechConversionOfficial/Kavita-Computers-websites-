## About the business

- **What it does:** Kavita Computers offers practical computer and IT training, with courses covering programming, office and accounting software, data science, AI, cybersecurity, cloud, networking and web development.
- **Who it serves:** students, job seekers, working professionals and learners seeking to develop practical digital skills.
- **Learning options:** the public website presents courses, certificates, internships, training batches and placement assistance.
- **Local context:** the institute's public materials identify Kalaburagi, Karnataka as its training-centre location.
- **Business background:** the About page describes a history dating to 2004. Figures for placements, students trained and accreditations are statements published by the institute, not independently audited findings of this repository.

## The website challenge

An IT training institute may offer dozens of programmes at different levels and durations. Prospective students need to find a course that fits their goals, compare the practical content and understand how to enrol. Internship seekers also need to distinguish internships from classroom training.

The website therefore needs to make a broad catalogue navigable while keeping the next step, whether browsing courses, asking about a batch or exploring internships, straightforward. This describes the user journey and the communication challenge the website addresses; it does **not** by itself establish measured problems with the earlier website.

## How the website presents the solution

1. **Start with career relevance.** The homepage positions the institute around practical, job-oriented training and gives visitors immediate routes to courses and enrolment.
2. **Organise a broad catalogue.** Course groups cover programming, office and accounting, data science and AI, cybersecurity, cloud, networking, design and related skills.
3. **Make course choice easier.** The visible course area presents programme names, level and duration information, with browsing and filtering options.
4. **Give internships their own space.** Finance and accounting, future skills, programming, web design and digital marketing internships are described separately from regular courses.
5. **Explain the learner journey.** The homepage introduces choosing a course, enrolling and learning, and working towards certification.
6. **Provide confidence-building information.** About, gallery and training-related sections present the institute's story, facilities and learning environment.
7. **Keep enquiries visible.** Enrolment and contact routes help prospective learners move from research to a conversation with the institute.

These observations describe the published website and project materials. They are not a verified record of the precise development tools, QA process or internal decisions made by Tech Conversion.
